# Trajectory Tracking for a Multicopter under a Quaternion Representation

# 1. Abstract

This project presents the design, implementation, and simulation of a nonlinear trajectory-tracking controller for a quadrotor UAV using differential flatness and quaternion-based attitude control.

The quadrotor is a nonlinear and underactuated aerial vehicle in which translational and rotational dynamics are strongly coupled. To address this problem, a hierarchical control architecture is developed. The outer-loop position controller tracks a desired three-dimensional trajectory and generates the required translational acceleration. A differential-flatness-based mapping is then used to determine the desired attitude and thrust. The inner-loop attitude controller uses quaternion representation and nonlinear computed-torque control to generate the required control torque.

The complete closed-loop system is implemented in MATLAB/Simulink. The model includes reference trajectory generation, position control, differential-flatness mapping, quaternion attitude control, translational dynamics, rotational dynamics, quaternion kinematics, feedback, and data logging.

A smooth quintic trajectory is generated over a 10-second simulation interval. The performance of the controller is evaluated using position tracking error, quaternion tracking error, thrust, control torque, and angular velocity.

The obtained position RMSE values are:

| Axis | RMSE |
|---|---:|
| X | 0.006659 m |
| Y | 0.004419 m |
| Z | 0.000041 m |

The quaternion tracking errors are:

| Component | RMSE |
|---|---:|
| $q_1$ | $1.6557 \times 10^{-4}$ |
| $q_2$ | $2.4949 \times 10^{-4}$ |
| $q_3$ | $1.2512 \times 10^{-19}$ |

The project also includes a MATLAB-based 3-D quadrotor animation that visualizes the actual vehicle position and attitude using the simulated quaternion state. MuJoCo is included as an additional physics-based simulation and visualization environment.

# 2. Introduction

Quadrotors are one of the most widely studied classes of unmanned aerial vehicles because of their mechanical simplicity, maneuverability, and ability to perform vertical take-off and landing.

However, controlling a quadrotor accurately is challenging because the system is nonlinear, underactuated, and dynamically coupled. Translational motion depends on vehicle orientation, while the orientation is controlled through body torques generated by the rotors.

For accurate trajectory tracking, the controller must therefore coordinate both translational and rotational motion.

This project investigates a hierarchical nonlinear control architecture based on differential flatness and quaternion-based attitude control.

The architecture separates the control problem into two main layers:

1. **Outer-loop position control**
2. **Inner-loop attitude control**

The position controller tracks the desired position and velocity and generates a desired translational acceleration. Differential flatness is then used to map this desired translational behavior to a desired attitude and thrust.

The attitude controller receives the desired quaternion and actual vehicle attitude and generates the required control torque.

The complete system is simulated in MATLAB/Simulink, while MuJoCo is considered as an additional physics-based validation and visualization environment.

---

# 3. Problem Statement

The objective is to design a closed-loop controller capable of making a quadrotor follow a smooth three-dimensional reference trajectory while maintaining the required attitude.

The controller should:

- track the desired $x, y, z$ position,
- minimize position tracking error,
- generate appropriate thrust,
- generate the desired vehicle attitude,
- track the desired quaternion,
- generate stabilizing control torques,
- maintain bounded angular velocity,
- preserve quaternion normalization,
- and demonstrate the complete behavior through simulation and visualization.

# 6. Mathematical Formulation

## 6.1 State Variables

Define:

$$
\xi =
\begin{bmatrix}
x\\
y\\
z
\end{bmatrix}
$$

as position,

$$
\dot{\xi} =
\begin{bmatrix}
\dot{x}\\
\dot{y}\\
\dot{z}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

as translational velocity,

$$
q =
\begin{bmatrix}
q_0\\
q_1\\
q_2\\
q_3
\end{bmatrix}
$$

as quaternion attitude, and

$$
\omega =
\begin{bmatrix}
\omega_x\\
\omega_y\\
\omega_z
\end{bmatrix}
$$

as body angular velocity.

Therefore, the complete state can be represented conceptually as:

$$
X =
\begin{bmatrix}
\xi\\
\dot{\xi}\\
q\\
\omega
\end{bmatrix}
$$

---

## 6.2 Translational Dynamics

The translational dynamics of the quadrotor are represented as:

$$
m\ddot{\xi} = TRe_3 - mge_3
$$

where

$$
e_3 =
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
0\\
1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Here:

- $m$ = quadrotor mass
- $\ddot{\xi}$ = translational acceleration
- $T$ = total thrust
- $R$ = rotation matrix corresponding to the vehicle attitude
- $g$ = gravitational acceleration
- $e_3$ = vertical unit vector

---

## 6.3 Rotational Dynamics

The rotational dynamics are given by:

$$
J\dot{\omega}
=
\tau - \omega \times (J\omega)
$$

and therefore:

$$
\dot{\omega}
=
J^{-1}
\left[
\tau - \omega \times (J\omega)
\right]
$$

where:

- $J$ = inertia matrix
- $\omega$ = body angular velocity
- $\tau$ = control torque

This represents the nonlinear rotational behavior of the quadrotor.

---

## 6.4 Quaternion Representation

The quadrotor attitude is represented using a scalar-first quaternion:

$$
q =
\begin{bmatrix}
q_0\\
q_1\\
q_2\\
q_3
\end{bmatrix}
$$

The quaternion must satisfy the unit-norm constraint:

$$
q_0^2 + q_1^2 + q_2^2 + q_3^2 = 1
$$

The quaternion normalization is implemented as:

$$
q_n = \frac{q}{\|q\|}
$$

This ensures that the attitude quaternion remains normalized during simulation.

---

## 6.5 Quaternion Kinematics

The quaternion kinematics are represented as:

$$
\dot{q}
=
\frac{1}{2}Q(q)\omega
$$

where $Q(q)$ is the quaternion kinematic matrix corresponding to the convention used in the implementation.

The quaternion kinematics subsystem updates the vehicle attitude based on the measured body angular velocity.

---

## 6.6 Reference Trajectory

A smooth three-dimensional reference trajectory is generated between the initial and final positions.

Initial position:

$$
p_0 =
\begin{bmatrix}
1.8\\
2.27\\
0.6
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Final position:

$$
p_f =
\begin{bmatrix}
2.9\\
3.0\\
1.2
\end{bmatrix}
$$

The trajectory uses a quintic polynomial:

$$
s(\tau)
=
10\tau^3 - 15\tau^4 + 6\tau^5
$$

with:

$$
\tau = \frac{t-t_0}{T}
$$

The desired position is:

$$
\xi_r(t)
=
p_0 + (p_f-p_0)s(\tau)
$$

The corresponding velocity is:

$$
\dot{\xi}_r
=
(p_f-p_0)\dot{s}
$$

and the acceleration is:

$$
\ddot{\xi}_r
=
(p_f-p_0)\ddot{s}
$$

Quintic interpolation is used because it provides zero initial and final velocity and acceleration, resulting in a smooth reference trajectory suitable for nonlinear trajectory tracking.

---

## 6.7 Position Controller

The position tracking error is defined as:

$$
e_\xi = \xi_r - \xi
$$

and the velocity tracking error is:

$$
e_{\dot{\xi}}
=
\dot{\xi}_r - \dot{\xi}
$$

The position controller generates the desired translational acceleration:

$$
\ddot{\xi}^{*}
=
\ddot{\xi}_r
+
K_{p,\xi}e_\xi
+
K_{i,\xi}\int e_\xi\,dt
+
K_{d,\xi}e_{\dot{\xi}}
$$

where:

- $K_{p,\xi}$ = proportional gain
- $K_{i,\xi}$ = integral gain
- $K_{d,\xi}$ = derivative gain
- $e_\xi$ = position error
- $e_{\dot{\xi}}$ = velocity error

---

## 6.8 Differential Flatness

Differential flatness provides the mapping between the desired translational motion and the required vehicle attitude and thrust.

The overall mapping is:

$$
\ddot{\xi}^{*}
\longrightarrow
(q_r,T)
$$

The `FlatnessMap` subsystem receives:

- Desired translational acceleration
- Desired yaw
- Quadrotor mass
- Gravitational acceleration

and generates:

- Desired quaternion $q_r$
- Required thrust $T$

This forms the connection between the outer-loop position controller and the inner-loop attitude controller.

---

## 6.9 Attitude Controller

The attitude controller compares the desired quaternion with the actual vehicle quaternion.

The quaternion error is defined as:

$$
q_e = q_r \otimes q^*
$$

where $\otimes$ represents quaternion multiplication and $q^*$ represents the quaternion conjugate.

The vector part of the quaternion error is:

$$
e_q = \operatorname{vec}(q_e)
$$

The attitude controller uses the quaternion error and angular velocity feedback to generate the required control torque:

$$
\tau = f(e_q,\omega)
$$

according to the implemented nonlinear computed-torque control law.

The generated torque is then applied to the quadrotor rotational dynamics to achieve the desired attitude.
